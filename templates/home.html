Challenge Information

NASA missions like Kepler and TESS have discovered many exoplanets, but sifting through the vast datasets is time-consuming, hindering rapid identification and classification. We are developing a machine learning model using publicly available data to automate and accelerate the discovery and classification of exoplanets, making this process more efficient and accessible to everyone.

What is an Exoplanet?

An exoplanet is a planet that exists outside of our solar system; meaning it orbits a star other than the Sun. While most exoplanets are found orbiting stars, some roam freely through space without being gravitationally bound to any star, and are called "rogue planets". As of today, NASA has confirmed the existence of over 6,000 exoplanets.

Our Model

Machine learning (ML) is a subfield of artificial intelligence (AI) focused on enabling systems to learn from data and improve performance on specific tasks without explicit programming. Our current ML system utilizes a Gradient Boosting Classifier, which enhances accuracy by sequentially combining predictions from multiple weaker decision trees. Our model, developed using this system and trained on NASA data, has achieved an accuracy of 90.4%.

How does this Model Work?

The ML problem for this challenge, the binary classification of planets as either candidates for being exoplanets or as false positives, involves the model handling tabular data. Historically, Gradient Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT) has been the one of the best options for such cases, winning multiple Kaggle competitions with problems similar in nature and beating deep neural networks such as CNN (Convolutional Neural Network).
